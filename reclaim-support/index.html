Reclaim Disk Space for macOS

Why does Reclaim ask for access to my disk?

App Store apps run in a sandbox and cannot read your files until you hand a location over. Choose a disk from the opening screen, or drag one onto the window, and macOS grants access to it. Reclaim remembers the grant so you only do this once.

Granting Full Disk Access in System Settings will not help — the sandbox takes precedence over it. This trips a lot of people up, and it is not something the app can change.

Some folders say they could not be read

macOS keeps a handful of locations private from every app. Their contents are still counted in the total; they just appear as Purgeable rather than under a name. You can grant access to a specific folder from Scan ▸ Choose Folder…

The total doesn't match Finder

Reclaim reports what files occupy, which is the same number du gives and the one that matters for free space. Finder sometimes shows the logical size instead, which differs for sparse disk images, hard links and compressed files.

What is "Purgeable"?

Space macOS is holding but will hand back when it needs to — mostly Time Machine local snapshots and iCloud files that have already been uploaded. Emptying the Trash and restarting usually releases it.

Is it safe to clear everything in the Reclaimable list?

Only the groups marked "Regenerates automatically" are ticked for you. Everything else is left unticked on purpose and explains what it costs. Either way, nothing is erased: items go to the Trash, so you can put anything back.

I bought Pro and it's not showing

Mac App Store version: open the palette menu in the toolbar and choose Unlock Pro…, then Restore Purchase. Pro is a one-time purchase tied to your Apple Account.

Direct download: choose Unlock Pro… then Enter Licence Key… and paste the key from your receipt email. The key is checked on your own Mac, so it keeps working offline and on every Mac you own. Lost it? Email below with the address you bought with.

Which version should I have?

The two are the same app. The direct download can be granted Full Disk Access, which lets it measure Mail, Safari data and Time Machine local snapshots — the App Store version cannot, because the sandbox overrides that setting. The App Store version updates itself through the App Store; the direct one updates itself in place.
